## Profile narrative

He was drawn to mathematics and the built environment at school, often noticing how roads, bridges and public buildings shaped the way people moved through their towns. He pursued civil engineering and began his career in India, gaining experience in site supervision and project coordination.

His move to Singapore exposed him to a highly organised urban infrastructure environment. The scale and density of the city demanded close coordination among engineers, contractors, planners and public agencies. He learned that successful engineering depends not only on design calculations but also on communication, documentation and the ability to anticipate how a project will be used over many years.

His responsibilities have included reviewing construction plans, coordinating site teams and resolving practical issues that arise when design meets real-world conditions. He places particular importance on safety procedures and on ensuring that technical decisions are understood by the people responsible for implementing them.

Arun has worked with multidisciplinary teams in which colleagues bring different professional training and cultural perspectives. He believes that a good project leader creates room for specialists to raise concerns early. Some of the most useful improvements, he says, emerge from conversations with people closest to the work rather than from formal presentations alone.

Urban infrastructure has also made him more attentive to sustainability. He is interested in the long-term performance of materials, efficient use of resources and the design of public spaces that remain practical as neighbourhoods change. He follows developments in transport and resilient infrastructure, particularly where engineering can improve everyday mobility.

Although Singapore is now home, Arun maintains close connections with Kerala and visits when work permits. He enjoys the contrast between the compact, carefully planned city in which he works and the more varied landscapes and settlement patterns of his home state. He also makes time for reading, photography and long walks.

Arun regards engineering as a public-facing profession, even when much of its work takes place behind the scenes. Roads, drainage systems and public structures are part of daily life for people who may never know who designed or coordinated them. He finds meaning in contributing to infrastructure that is safe, dependable and useful over time.
