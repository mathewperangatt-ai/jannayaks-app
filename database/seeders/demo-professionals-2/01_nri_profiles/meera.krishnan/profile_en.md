## Profile introduction
Meera Krishnan is a public-health researcher based in London. Her work examines how health services can better understand the needs of diverse communities and how evidence can be translated into practical improvements in access, communication and care.

## Profile narrative
Meera grew up in Kerala in a family where books, discussion and education were part of everyday life. She developed an interest in the social dimensions of health while studying life sciences, becoming increasingly curious about why access to care differs between communities even when services are formally available.

After completing her higher education in India, she pursued further research training in the United Kingdom. The transition introduced her to new academic methods and to the importance of working across disciplines. She found that public health questions rarely belong to one field alone; they often require contributions from medicine, social science, statistics and community organisations.

Her research work involves reviewing evidence, analysing service experiences and contributing to studies on health inequalities. She is particularly interested in the gap between policy intentions and the everyday experience of people trying to use healthcare services. She believes that research is most useful when it listens carefully to the people affected by the systems being studied.

Meera has collaborated with academic colleagues and community partners on projects that involve interviews, evidence reviews and the interpretation of findings. She values careful methodology, but also believes that research communication should be accessible beyond specialist audiences. A finding that remains buried in a technical report has limited practical reach.

Working in an international academic environment has helped her understand how migration, language and social context influence health experiences. She is attentive to the different ways people describe illness and navigate unfamiliar institutions. These perspectives have strengthened her interest in inclusive research and culturally sensitive public communication.

She maintains a close relationship with Kerala and follows public-health developments in India. She enjoys reading, visiting museums and libraries, and taking walks through London's neighbourhoods. Malayalam literature remains an important part of her personal reading, providing a connection to language and memory.

Meera hopes to continue work that connects research with practical service improvement. She is motivated by the possibility that careful evidence, communicated clearly and developed with communities, can make institutions more responsive. For her, academic work is not only about producing publications; it is also about helping people ask better questions and make better-informed decisions.
